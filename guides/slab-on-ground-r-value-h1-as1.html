Slab-on-ground R-values: the Appendix E lookup, step by step

There is no layer-by-layer calculation for a concrete slab on ground. H1/AS1 6th edition gives its construction R-value in 24 tables in Appendix E — and which number you read depends on four things: the slab's shape, the foundation, the insulation and the external wall.

1. Measure the area and the perimeter

Use the internal dimensions of the heated slab: Equation E.1 is simply A/P = area ÷ perimeter. Two rules decide what goes in:

  • Leave out the garage, porch and any other unconditioned slab when measuring the area.
  • Keep in the perimeter the wall between the house and the garage (E.1.2.2) — heat still leaves the slab there.

Measuring on external dimensions instead? Equation E.2 takes A/P − w/2, where w is the effective wall thickness in metres. For a 140 m² slab with a 52 m perimeter and a 100 mm wall, that is 2.69 − 0.05 = 2.64 — the same table row either way here.

Two heated slabs that don't touch, with an unconditioned area between them, are calculated separately (E.1.2.2 Comment 3).

2. Find the row — the smaller one

The tables have rows for A/P from 0.6 up to 10.0 and above. Between two rows you use the smaller A/P — there is no interpolation. 140 ÷ 52 = 2.69, so you read the 2.6 row.

Shape matters more than you might expect. For a conventional slab with no insulation and a light-clad wall:

SlabA/PRowR
90 m², 40 m perimeter2.252.2R0.9
140 m², 52 m perimeter2.692.6R1.1
200 m², 62 m perimeter3.233.2R1.2

A bigger, squarer slab has less edge per square metre, and most of a slab's heat loss is at the edge.

3. Pick the table

The 24 tables cover every combination of:

  • Foundation — a conventional slab with footings, or a concrete raft (waffle / pods)
  • Vertical edge insulation — none or R1.0
  • Underslab insulation — none, a 1.2 m perimeter strip of R1.2 or R2.4, or full cover R1.2 or R2.4 (slab floors only)
  • External wall — masonry veneer or other cladding

Within the table, the column is the effective wall thickness band: 90–140, 140–180, 180–250, 250–300 or 300 mm and over — the horizontal distance from the outer edge of the slab to the inside face of the wall. It matters most on insulated slabs: with R1.0 edge and full R2.4 underslab, the slab below goes from R2.3 at 100 mm to R2.8 at 300 mm and over.

Two things stand out. Edge insulation and underslab insulation together do far more than either alone. And for this shape, an uninsulated raft reads higher than an uninsulated conventional slab — that is how the tables are published.

Where it catches people out

  • Pods are not insulation. A raft without added insulation is read from the uninsulated raft tables.
  • A raft with underslab insulation has no table. Insulation under or within a raft, a raft on piles, insulation over the slab or a topping slab, and edge insulation other than R1.0 all need a value calculated to H1/VM1 Appendix E — usually from the slab system's manufacturer (E.1.1.1 b)).
  • More than R1.0 at the edge adds little (E.1.2.1 Comment 4).
  • Old guide values don't count. Slab values from the BRANZ House Insulation Guide 5th edition or earlier are not acceptable (E.1.1.1 Comment 1).
  • Carpet and other floor coverings are not counted (2.1.3.1 a) iii)).
  • The tables assume soil conductivity λ = 2.0 W/(m·K) (E.1.2.1 Comment 5).

When the edge isn't the same all the way round

Most houses have a stretch of perimeter without edge insulation — the wall to the garage, or a door or full-height window opening with nothing under it, which counts as "no edge insulation" perimeter (E.1.2.3 Comment). Equations E.3–E.5 combine the parts by perimeter length:

R = 1 / Σ (Pi/P ÷ Ri)

The 140 m² slab with R1.0 edge insulation, except 6 m of house–garage wall without it:

1/R = (46/52) ÷ 1.3 + (6/52) ÷ 1.1 → R = 1.27

Six metres of uninsulated edge takes the slab from R1.3 to R1.27. Thermally separating the garage slab with vertical edge insulation is recommended (E.1.2.1 Comment 1).

Is there a minimum?

Not for an ordinary slab: 2.1.2.11 sets no minimum for slab-on-ground, and its R-value goes into the heat-loss calculation with everything else. A heated slab — embedded heating — must reach the Table 2.1.2.12 minimum: R2.5 in zones 1–3, R2.8 in zone 4 and R3.0 in zones 5–6. For the slab above, the strongest table combination — R1.0 edge plus full R2.4 underslab — gives R2.3 with a 100 mm effective wall, short of R2.5. The same combination reads R2.5 in the 180–250 mm band and R2.8 at 300 mm and over. Where the tables fall short, a heated slab needs an H1/VM1 Appendix E calculation.

These figures are read from the Appendix E tables as transcribed in the calculator. Check them against your own copy of H1/AS1 before they go into a consent. The calculator does not decide compliance — the building consent authority does.
